## Helicopters

## Building a helicopter

A minimal standard helicopter requires:

1. An [engine](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Guides/Engine) to provide power
2. A main [rotor](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Propulsion) to turn power into vectored thrust on the vertical plane.
3. A tail rotor to turn power into vectored thrust on the horizontal plane, including correcting for the spin generated by the main rotor
4. A [pilot seat](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/User_Input) to allow the pilot to start the engine, control the throttle, and steer
5. A [gyroscope](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Logic) to stabilize the vehicle's controls, allowing for much easier hovering and flight
6. Low enough weight to take off

Stormworks provides a starter helicopter preset which can be loaded in the editor to save time, or you can continue reading to learn how to make your own from scratch.

### Center of mass

[![Sample Heli Frame](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/e/eb/Sample_Heli_Frame.png/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/492?cb=20190217202729)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/e/eb/Sample_Heli_Frame.png/revision/latest?cb=20190217202729)

The [center of mass](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Center_of_Mass) (CoM) should be as close to directly under the rotor as possible for efficient hovering. Even being offset by 1-2 blocks ahead or behind will significantly worsen stability and performance. This is because correcting for the offset CoM requires rotor pitch, which creates drag, which lowers engine efficiency, worsening lifting power and increasing fuel consumption. The extra drag produced by the main rotor also increases the torque it produces on the helicopter body, requiring extra force from the the tail rotor to counteract. This contributes to sideways drifting. Further, the combined inefficiency can prevent a low-torque engine from running at its RPS limiter setting, which means RPS may dip down when the helicopter attempts to maneuver, causing the required pitch input to increase, thus increasing the required tail rotor correction, leading to further RPS drop and overall 'spazzy' or 'jerky' movement.

If the CoM can't be directly underneath the rotor and you have to choose between a little ahead or a little behind, it is better for the CoM to be a little ahead of the rotor. This is because when the helicopter pitches to move forward, the CoM will come underneath it, so the slightly forward CoM will improve efficiency and stability in forward flight.

Note that since the weight of the helicopter needs to be minimized to have maximum lift and fuel efficiency, it is quite undesirable to use [weighted blocks](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Blocks). Even just a few can cause noticeably worsened performance. Instead, the rotor should be repositioned forward or backward as necessary, and the tail extended or shortened as necessary to allow for the main rotor adjustment.

### [Engines](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Guides/Engine)

The simplest way to power a helicopter is with a built-in diesel engine.

The engine's power port must be connected to the rotor and tail rotor with pipes. A gearbox can be placed to convert torque into RPS, though the gear ratios should be kept low enough so that the engine can run at its full RPS limiter setting in-flight, ensuring that it can always provide a little bit more power if necessary to maintain stable RPS.

The engine also receives Logic Data connections.

Since all the basic axis controls of the device are often taken by steering the helicopter (W/S, A/D, Left/Right, and Up/Down), a simple design to control the engine's throttle is to place a [throttle](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/User_Input) component in the helicopter, then use the throttle output from that to control the engine's throttle. The inputs for the throttle component can then be controlled using hotkeys from the pilot's seat.

1. Click 'Logic' at the top and then the 'Data' icon on the left to enter the Logic Data screen
2. Drag from 'Throttle Value' output on the throttle component to the 'Throttle' input on the engine (we will connect the throttle component to the pilot's seat later)

[![Helicopter Throttle Engine Connection](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/8/83/Helicopter_Throttle_Engine_Connection.png/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/663?cb=20190217202901)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/8/83/Helicopter_Throttle_Engine_Connection.png/revision/latest?cb=20190217202901)

### Main Rotor

The main rotor is the primary means of controlling the helicopter and is what provides [lift](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Lift) to the vehicle, allowing it to fly. The rotor takes its inputs from the gyro, which automatically stabilizes the helicopter. Without the gyro, the helicopter can fly barrel rolls and loops. With it, the helicopter should be able to hover almost effortlessly on the part of the player, and pitching forward will just hold the helicopter at a forward tilt, converting the rotor's thrust into forward movement.

The main rotor should be placed according to where the center of mass of the vehicle is, above the vehicle. If the rotor is overlapping any other components (including the tail rotor) it cannot be placed.

The main rotor has 3 inputs: Pitch, Roll, and Collective. In this simple design, collective can be thought of as 'lift' or 'up/down'. It is also sometimes known as "collective pitch" or "thrust control", but this is dependent on how it is used.

1. Click 'Logic' at the top and then the 'Data' icon on the left to enter the Logic Data screen
2. Drag from the gyroscope's 'Stabilised Pitch' output to the 'Pitch' input on the main rotor
3. Drag from the gyroscope's 'Stabilised Roll' output to the 'Roll' input on the main rotor
4. Drag from the gyroscope's 'Stabilised Up/Down' output to the 'Collective' input on the main rotor

[![Gyro Main Rotor Connections](data:image/gif;base64,R0lGODlhAQABAIABAAAAAP///yH5BAEAAAEALAAAAAABAAEAQAICTAEAOw%3D%3D)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/3/30/Gyro_Main_Rotor_Connections.png/revision/latest?cb=20190217203504)

Since we are using the collective in this design, it is also necessary to ensure that the Main Rotor's blade pitch is neutral. To do this, click 'Select', click the Main Rotor, then set the Blade Pitch to Neutral.

Note that by default, a rotor spins clockwise when viewed top-down. As a result, the rotor induces a counter-clockwise force on the helicopter body, causing the helicopter to yaw left. However, if the rotor is inverted (e.g. with the 'u' key in the editor) it will rotate counter-clockwise and induce clockwise force, yawing right. This can be useful for making helicopters without tail rotors, e.g. [tandem rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandem-rotor_aircraft) or [coaxial rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxial-rotor_aircraft) helicopters.

### Tail Rotor

The tail rotor complements the main rotor by providing a corrective force on the horizontal plane. The tail rotor is normally set to allow the helicopter to spin positively or negatively by accepting a yaw input from the gyroscope. In simple terms, it allows the helicopter to 'turn'.

Like the main rotor, the tail rotor accepts inputs from the gyroscope and works best with its Blade Pitch set to Neutral. For a normally-configured main rotor, the tail rotor should stick out on the left side of the tail, and it will rotate clockwise when viewed from the left of the helicopter.

1. Click 'Logic' at the top and then the 'Data' icon on the left to enter the Logic Data screen
2. Drag from the gyroscope's 'Stabilised Yaw' output to the 'Yaw' input on the tail rotor

[![Gyro to rotor yaw](data:image/gif;base64,R0lGODlhAQABAIABAAAAAP///yH5BAEAAAEALAAAAAABAAEAQAICTAEAOw%3D%3D)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/c/ce/Gyro_to_rotor_yaw.png/revision/latest?cb=20190217203210)

Note that it's best to place tail rotors up high, close to or preferably at the same level as the main rotor. This helps to ensure that the tail rotor's force effectively counters the force created by the main rotor without inducing roll. If roll is induced by an excessively low tail rotor, it will cause the main rotor to need to counter the roll, which increases drag, lowers maximum lift and fuel economy, and further increases the force the tail rotor needs to counter, inducing more roll, etc. A low tail rotor is also at increased risk of a tail strike during landing or takeoff which can damage it and cause extreme instability in flight.

### Gyroscope

The gyroscope in Stormworks is a component that provides adjustments to the controls entered by the pilot to make them suitable for controlling the various inputs of the main rotor. It takes its inputs from the pilot's seat, whilst providing outputs to the rotors.

The gyro offers an 'auto-hover' functionality, which can be controlled via a toggle input and assists the pilot is maintaining a hover in the air. It's very useful for vertical take offs and landings, and for players without joysticks it can be a helpful tool in level flight as well.

### Pilot Seat

A [pilot seat](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/User_Input) is the easiest way of setting up a simple helicopter using keyboard controls. To use a joystick or gamepad, the [Pilot Seat (HOTAS)](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/User_Input) can be used instead.

A pilot seat, once placed, needs to be connected in the Logic Data screen to the components which it controls.

For a basic design, W/S is used to control pitch, A/D is used to control roll, Up/Down is used to control the collective (up/down), while Left/Right is set to control the yaw. It is highly recommended these are all set of 'Reset' Mode using the 'Select' tool.

Using the Select Tool, the pilot seat's Hotkey 1 can be set from 'push' to 'toggle' and used to control the on/off state of the engines. Hotkey 2 and 3 can be used to control the throttle for the engines (in push mode), whilst Hotkey 4 is used to control the gyroscope's auto-hover (in toggle mode).

It is highly recommended to go in to the 'Select' tool, click the pilot seat, and name all of the controls. This will cause the game to display the controls in the bottom left when in-game, allowing the pilot to see at a glance how to control the vehicle. It can also be helpful for the designer, you, if you come back to the design later and need to remember how it works.

1. Go to the Logic Data screen and drag from Axis 1 (A/D) to the gyroscope's Roll input
2. Drag from Axis 2 (W/S) to the gyroscope's Pitch input
3. Drag from Axis 3 (Left/Right) to the gyroscope's Up/Down input
4. Drag from Axis 4 (Up/Down) to the gyroscope's Yaw input
5. Drag from Hotkey 1 (Engine Start) to the engine's On/Off input
6. Drag from Hotkey 2 (Throttle Up) to the throttle component's Up input
7. Drag from Hotkey 3 (Throttle Down) to the throttle component's Down input
8. Drag from Hotkey 4 (Auto-hover) to the gyroscope's Auto-hover input

### Troubleshooting

- If the engine won't start (makes no start sound), make sure to set the engine on/off to a toggle button or hotkey on the pilot's seat.
- If the engine starts but the rotors won't spin when throttle is applied, make sure that the throttle is connected to the pilot's seat and to the engines. Also ensure that the rotors are receiving power from the engines. It is also be worth checking that throttle is connected the right way around (try putting the throttle in reverse to test this).
- If the controls are not working as expected ensure they are connected to the right gyroscope data connections, and that the rotors are only accepting 'stabilised' inputs from the gyroscope. Also check that auto-hover is not enabled.
- If the rotors are spinning but the helicopter won't take off when you press up/down, and you're sure it's connected correctly, it could be that your helicopter is too heavy.
- If you take off but the helicopter spins uncontrollably, it is likely your tail rotor is not connected correctly.
- If your helicopter tends to tilt in a particular direction without the pilot's input check the center of mass for your vehicle is directly underneath your rotors. Any deviation can be corrected using [trim](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Trim) (brought up using the ALT key on Windows).
- If the helicopter is working but can be a bit hard to fly, congratulations! Your helicopter is working, and flying a helicopter with a keyboard, no flight computer, and no practice is hard. Don't expect to fly like the Night Stalkers[[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/160th_Special_Operations_Aviation_Regiment) immediately.

## Next Steps

Common requirements for a helicopter:

- HOTAS! Install a [Pilot Seat (HOTAS)](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/User_Input) to get better controls that don't rely on the keyboard
- Displays. Installing displays in the cockpit can help the pilot fly better in first-person. Suggestions include the [Artificial Horizon](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Displays), a Throttle [Dial](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Displays), a Speed [Dial](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Displays), a [Compass Sensor](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Sensors), an [Altimeter](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Sensors), and a [Wind Sensor](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Sensors).
- More power! Installing more engines allows you to carry more weight on the vehicle and move faster. Don't hesistate to adjust the sensitivity and maximums of some of the components to retain the ability to control it, however.
- Co-pilot! You can use [numeric switchboxes](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Logic) to allow a co-pilot's seat to be connected and allow control to be transferred between the pilot and co-pilot using a [Toggle Button](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/User_Input). You could also build a [microcontroller](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Logic) for this task.
- More seats! Place [passenger seats](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Person_Operations) to allow passengers to be safely transported.
- More lights! Add [lights](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Lights) for when it gets dark, or you won't be able to see what's happening.
