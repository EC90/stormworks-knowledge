## Engine

[![Engine Description](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/2/29/Engine_Description.png/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/180?cb=20190217210136)](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/stormworks_gamepedia_en/images/2/29/Engine_Description.png/revision/latest?cb=20190217210136)

The term engine describes any object which provides [mechanical power](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Mechanical_Power) when provided with [fuel](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Fuel), and something to burn fuel with, like air or oxygen. As well as fluid to cool the engine.

This article looks at the Engine component, a small diesel engine (not to be confused with the [Diesel Engine](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Propulsion) also known as the *large* diesel engine).

The small diesel engine provides mechanical power to other components. It accepts a throttle input, allowing the output of the engine to be controlled. It is often the first component placed on any design after basic blocks.

It is commonly paired with a [Wiki/Guides/Gearbox](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Guides/Gearbox) and [clutch](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Propulsion) to provide a full drivetrain. It is possible for the engine to overheat, so it requires a coolant (typically [water](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Fluids)) to be pumped in and out of the engine (which is supported by the engine's own suction). The engine requires an in-take of air, and it also requires [diesel fuel](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Game_Mechanics/Fuel) to operate. Finally, an [exhaust](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Wiki/Building/Components/Fluids) is required to pump out the fumes created from the engine's operation.
