# Gameplay/Gamemodes/Classic

In classic game mode, the player starts off with limited money, parts, and fuel. Money must be earned from doing missions to afford to build vehicles, buy fuel, and buy new outposts. Research points must be obtained by collecting [loot crates](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Loot_crate) or completing missions and can spent on [research](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Gameplay/Research) to unlock parts. Note that this mode can be a good place to start for a new player since it can teach the basics with simple blocks and gradually add new blocks over time to make the game's extensive parts list less overwhelming.

The map starts off unexplored. The player has access to both first-person and third-person view. Respawning is available. The player cannot teleport, not even between player-owned bases.

## Starting location

Various starting locations can be chosen. Without the Industrial Frontier DLC, the default starting location is [Coastguard Outpost Beginner](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Coastguard_Outpost_Beginner), a small island with a small dock where you can build your ship.

If the player owns the Industrial Frontier DLC, the default starting location is [North Meier Outpost](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/North_Meier_Outpost). Similar to the Coastguard Outpost Beginner, this outpost offers a small dock to build your ship as well as a garage for small road vehicles and VTOLs.

## Gameplay

You'll frequently be offered missions and this is your main way of getting new blocks and materials as well as earn some cash. Alternatively, you can find loot out in the world, either floating in the ocean or on islands.

As you progress in the game, you'll discover new islands you'll be able to purchase if you have enough money, these islands will offer the ability to fast travel to them and offer a new building location. Depending on the island you bought you will be able to build larger ships, or air and ground vehicles, assuming you have the necessary building blocks.

New and more difficult missions will be offered as you unlock more islands. You can look on the map to see what missions are available so you can prepare and alter your vehicle if needed.

## Starting resources

The player starts off with $15,000 and 5 research points. There should also be 2,110L of jet fuel, 5,110L of diesel, and 100 units of coal at the starting outpost.

- Pipes of all of the various types (including enclosed pipes).
- [Windows](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Gameplay/Workbench/Components/Windows) of all of the various sizes and shapes (excluding portholes)
- Small [diesel engine](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Gameplay/Workbench/Components/Propulsion/DieselEngines)
- Small propeller
- Small electric motor
- Small generator
- Clutch
- Gearbox
- [Small battery](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Electric_battery_small)
- Electric circuit breaker
- Rudder and fin rudder
- Basic display parts, including the dial, digital display, light, and indicator light
- A searchlight
- Basic controls, including the throttle lever, push button, and toggle button
- Control seats, including the compact pilot seat, driver seat, pilot seat, pilot seat (HOTAS), saddle seat, and control handle.
- Passenger seats, including the passenger seat, saddle passenger seat, padded seat
- A bed
- Many 'fluid' parts, including the exhaust, fluid heat radiator, fluid port/port end, fluid tank small/medium, gas/liquid relief valves, and all gas tank sizes.
- Various specialist equipment including binoculars (and night vision binoculars), compass, defibrillator, dog whistle, fire extinguisher, first aid kit, fishing rod, flare, flaregun ammo, flaregun, flashlight, glowstick, oxygen mask, radio signal locator, radio, remote control unit, strobe light (and infrared version), transponder, welding torch (and underwater version)
- Ropes, cables, and fluid hoses
- Empty inventory spots for small equipment, large equipment, and outfits

- Manual control parts, including the torque crank and fluid pump (manual)
- A few sensors, including the gas meter, barometer, torque meter, laser sensor (missile)
- Several connectors, including the electric connector, fluid connector, torque connector, small connector, and all three hardpoint types.
- 'Anchors', including the rope anchor, fluid hose anchor, and electric cable anchor
- Wheels, but only the 3x3 wheel and the wheel coaster

- Small turret ring
- Friction pad
- Flags (small/medium/large)
- RX Directional and RX Directional (Large), long-range directional radios useful for communicating with spacecraft in orbit.

## Resource usage

Player vehicles can spawn with fuel automatically taken from the outpost's reserves. However, note that when a vehicle is despawned, any fuel it contains will not be automatically returned to the outpost. To keep the fuel, the vehicle must first be connected to the outpost's fuel supply stations with a fuel hose, and then the station must be set to pump the fuel back in. Ensure that the fuel hose is also disconnected and restocked on the pump before despawning the vehicle or it will be lost.
