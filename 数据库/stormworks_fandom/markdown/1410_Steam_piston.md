# Steam piston

Steam pistons are used to make steam engines for power production fueled by coal, crude oil, or nuclear fuel rods.

## Basics

Steam pistons do not require any valves for basic operation. Simply direct high-pressure steam from a [boiler](https://stormworks.fandom.com/wiki/Boiler) into their steam inlets and then allow the steam from their outlets to either vent out to the atmosphere or go into condensers. To avoid having your pistons get stuck (and to smooth out your power production), adjust the pistons in select mode so they do not at all have the same 'position' setting, preferably with an even spacing between the set positions (e.g. -0.5, -0.25, 0, 0.25).

Note that the default position setting of 0 for all pistons will lead to a stuck steam engine if it is not changed.

Also note that, unlike in real life, pressurized air cannot be used to turn steam turbines or operate steam pistons.

## Troubleshooting

### Negative RPS

If steam out and steam in are reversed, steam pistons will provide negative RPS. If connected to a generator, this will still generate power without any problems but may seem odd if the player inspects the piston and sees high outlet pressure and low inlet pressure. However, if connected to a propeller or wheel, this will provide reverse thrust without requiring a gearbox (thus improving efficiency). Valves can be used to switch the steam inlets and outlets to intentionally use this effect as desired.

### Low boiler pressure

A boiler pressure below 58 atm generally indicates inadequate water supply or low temperature and will result in less-than-ideal power production from your pistons. If the boiler is not 'full' (steam + water = 175), the problem is not enough water. To fix this, you should use condensers to convert your steam back to fresh water. Additionally, a medium fluid tank of fresh water placed before the large pump feeding into the boiler is generally enough extra water to keep the system at full pressure.

Steam engines can work with just the water that spawns in the boiler, but the pressure tends to end up lower than maximum (e.g. 40-50 atm instead of 60) because of the steam in the pistons and water+steam in the condenser.
